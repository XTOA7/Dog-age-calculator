How the calculator works

The old rule of "multiply by 7" is a myth. Dogs mature much faster than humans in their first two years. A 1-year-old dog is roughly equivalent to a 15-year-old human, and a 2-year-old dog is like a 24-year-old human.

After the age of 2, the aging process slows down, but it depends heavily on the size of the dog. Small dogs live longer and age slower (adding about 4 human years per dog year). Giant dogs age much faster (adding 6-7 human years per dog year).

Life Stages at a Glance

  • Puppy (0-1 years): Rapid growth, lots of sleep, high energy, needs training and vaccinations.
  • Adult (1-7 years): Prime health, settled behavior, peak energy. Preventative vet care is key.
  • Senior (7+ years): Slower pace, potential joint stiffness, weight gain, and a need for softer beds.

Frequently asked questions

Why do small dogs live longer than large dogs?

Veterinary science shows that larger bodies grow faster and accumulate cellular damage more quickly, leading to earlier onset of age-related diseases like arthritis and cancer. Small dogs often live to 14-16 years, while giant breeds often live 8-10 years.

When is a dog considered a senior?

For small and medium breeds, it's usually around 7-8 years. For large and giant breeds, they are often considered seniors by 5 or 6 years old. You should ask your vet about switching to senior food and more frequent checkups at this stage.
